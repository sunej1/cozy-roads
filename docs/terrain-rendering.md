# Rendering a mountain from the driver's seat

## What the first comparison showed

At the same road position on seed 83472, the old camera stopped at 1.74 km. The fog already covered 94% of the terrain color by 1.42 km. A more detailed rock texture could change the nearest cliff. The range behind that cliff had to exist before it could cast a shadow, appear in the lake, or come into view around a bend.

## How much of Nanite makes sense here?

Epic selects clusters from a hierarchy of simplified triangle meshes using their projected error. Its renderer also handles visibility, fine triangles and streaming for arbitrary authored meshes. Scthe demonstrated meshlet selection, occlusion culling and software rasterization in a real WebGPU browser demo. Its headline scene counts include many instances of the same source meshes. The author's account of packing depth and normals into a 32-bit word explains one practical cost of that rasterizer. Those counts are useful evidence of feasibility and a poor forecast for a unique mountain range. [Epic's SIGGRAPH presentation](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Karis_Nanite_SIGGRAPH_Advances_2021_final.pdf) · [Browser demo and implementation notes](https://github.com/Scthe/nanite-webgpu)

The road, tires, lake and grass all want to know the elevation at a point. A height field supplies that answer directly. Geometry clipmaps demonstrate how to keep a moving window of terrain resident. Mapbox’s MARTINI measures relief during simplification. A triangle spanning a plain and one spanning a knife-edge ridge can cover the same area and introduce very different height errors. [Losasso and Hoppe](https://hhoppe.com/proj/geomclipmap/) · [MARTINI and interactive demo](https://github.com/mapbox/martini)

Cozy Roads builds three index sets per 128 m strip. It measures the height missed by coarser triangles against the page's sampled grid, then projects that difference into pixels using camera field of view, framebuffer height and distance. Nearby mountain pages have a finer source grid. Adjacent strips retain their shared edge samples, including the border of the driving mesh. The 2.2-pixel threshold on Standard and Cinematic guides selection. It is an estimate against sampled heights and does not bound the error against continuous terrain. Selection runs on the CPU and Three.js submits indexed triangles to either graphics backend. A general meshlet rasterizer would become interesting for overhangs, caves or dense authored cliff scans, which a height field cannot represent.

## What gives the range its shape?

Schott and colleagues amplify coarse terrain with erosion at several scales. Rune Skovbo Johansen's 2026 erosion experiments show how details aligned with the slope produce recognizable drainage. Michael Kinsey demonstrated an adaptation for Unreal's Landmass plugin. The first procedural pass in this project made sharper summits, but produced repetitive faces. The current source field uses a public survey so drainage, saddles and buttresses already relate to one another. [Terrain Amplification Using Multi-scale Erosion](https://aparis69.github.io/public_html/projects/schott2024_Erosion.html) · [Johansen's erosion experiments](https://blog.runevision.com/2026/03/fast-and-gorgeous-erosion-filter.html) · [Kinsey's X post, linked from Johansen's write-up](https://x.com/MichaelKinsey3D/status/2043407307984523608)

The source is a 768 × 768 USGS 3DEP elevation export around Mount Shuksan, Washington. Its geographic bounds are 121.70–121.50° W and 48.762–48.896° N. The export used UTM zone 10N, bilinear sampling and approximately 19.75 m grid spacing. The bundled raster stores little-endian unsigned heights in quarter metres. Seeded rotations, warping, blending and scaling produce fictional, reproducible ranges. The same elevations feed driving and rendering. Surface shading also reads the difference between the survey and a smoothed version of it. Concave terrain can retain snow lower down the face, and the fine slope survives when geometry simplifies. [USGS 3DEP elevation service](https://elevation.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevation/ImageServer)

## Why light matters kilometres away

Fritsch and colleagues' 2025 work stores a terrain horizon over many sun directions, so an offscreen ridge can still block sunlight. This game's fixed sun only needs one direction. A sweep through a 15.36 km height field records the blocking elevation. A surface compares its height with that value to decide how much direct sun reaches it. A coarse field gives the effect a penumbra and can miss narrow spires. The normal shadow map still supplies car, tree and roadside shadows. [Fast Planetary Shadows Using Fourier-Compressed Horizon Maps](https://www.cg.cs.tu-bs.de/upload/publications/fritsch2025fchorizonmaps/fritsch2025fchorizonmaps.pdf)

Johansen's field photographs show lit and shaded mountain faces at the same distance taking on different colors. Heckel's browser experiments show how integrating density along a ray gives a cloud depth and how sampling toward the sun shades its interior. The sky integrates 18 samples through a moving cloud layer. Each sample reads density and precomputed sunlight from the same volume. Run `node scripts/bake-clouds.mjs` to regenerate it after changing the cloud shape or sun direction. The bake also integrates sunlight through the volume into a 2D shadow map. Each receiver projects onto that map at its own elevation. This lets a band of light move across kilometers of forest and snow. A sample of the terrain shadow between the camera and each ridge controls how much the low-altitude haze warms in sunlight. [Field photographs and atmospheric perspective](https://blog.runevision.com/2025/06/notes-on-atmospheric-perspective-and.html) · [Heckel's WebGL cloud experiments](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/real-time-cloudscapes-with-volumetric-raymarching/)

Two recent X demos point to a different lighting improvement. Maxime Heckel showed the sky contributing to indirect illumination and reflections as the sun moves. Cody Bennett showed glossy single-bounce lighting reconstructed from radiance cascades. Both are relevant to tunnels or a car parked beside a brightly lit wall. In the fixed road screenshots, the bigger missing cue was a distant ridge interrupting direct sunlight. This pass implements that cue and keeps the existing screen-space bounce in Cinematic. Heckel reproduces both posts and explains the probe experiment in his November 2026 write-up. [Heckel's X demo](https://x.com/MaximeHeckel/status/2112909030318551231) · [Bennett's X demo](https://x.com/CodyBennett/status/2101979551500816730) · [Technical write-up and embedded posts](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/lighting-that-feels-just-right/)

## Verification

At 1.67 km along the original road on seed 83472, terrain-only camera rays intersected mountains as far away as 6.11 km. All three detail levels were active. The seam test compares the vertices referenced by the actual draw indices of adjacent mountain pages at different levels, then checks the same edge against a driving tile.

On the M2 MacBook Air, Chrome averaged 48.5 FPS over the 30-second, 600 m opening drive on seed 83472 in Cinematic at 2160 × 1350. The sparser meadow averaged 53.0 FPS on the same route. Both runs stayed at full resolution and measured 33.4 ms at the 95th percentile. In a separate WebGL 2 Standard run at 1280 × 800 on seed 275, the updated renderer averaged 60.0 FPS with a 16.8 ms 95th percentile.
